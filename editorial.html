How we work

Editorial policy

How VisiDocsverse articles are chosen, researched and checked.

This page is the public version of the standard every article on this site must pass. If an article cannot meet it, it does not ship.

One keyword, one page

Each article targets exactly one search question and must differ in intent angle from everything else on the site — no near-duplicates, no cannibalization. Slugs carry the intent angle, not just the topic.

SERP-checked or skipped

Before writing, we check what already ranks on page one for the keyword. The draft must beat it — covering what top results cover, plus the gap they miss. If page one is excellent and unbeatable, we skip the keyword and take the next one. No thin filler, ever.

Real citations only

External claims cite real sources with verbatim URLs the author is certain exist: scholarly publishing (DOI, arXiv, JSTOR), HCI and visualization research (ACM, IEEE), research institutions (Nature, Science, NIH), citation standards (APA), digital preservation (Library of Congress), and web and cryptography standards (W3C, NIST). Fewer real citations always beat fabricated ones — any URL that cannot be verified is dropped, and invented URLs are a hard fail.

Answer first, no filler

Direct answer in the first sentences, key takeaways right after the intro, one idea per paragraph, a FAQ of real reader questions at the end. Cut repetition. Every sentence complete — model cutoffs get re-run, never shipped.

Authorship

Articles are written by the VisiDocsverse team with a three-stage pipeline — researcher (brief), writer (draft), optimizer (QA edit) — and every internal link points at a real page on this site, every download or signup call-to-action at our public checkout or the public trial page. Nothing gated, nothing invented.

Corrections

Found an error? Tell us through the contact section on the homepage — verified corrections are fixed in the article and noted with a revision date.

Cadence

At most one article per day, staged ahead and shipped slowly. The queue head is always next — never cherry-picked.
