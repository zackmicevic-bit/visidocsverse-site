Tags View: Your Labels, Mapped

Real recording from the VisiDocsverse desktop app · 10-second loop · No mockups

Above: one click on TAGS — the corpus re-webs around labels, with the ALL TAGS panel open over all 37 pills.

Labels become a web

Tags usually live in sidebars: little pills you click one at a time, blind to how they relate. In TAGS view every label is a body — glowing orbs joined wherever two labels share documents, so your whole taxonomy draws itself live. The footage above shows the flip: the corpus re-webs around its labels while the PROJECT TAG panel lists the member documents of the selected tag. Where the web is dense, your vocabulary is working; where a body floats alone, you have found an orphan — or your next insight.

How a tags map works

  • One click to TAGS: MAIN shows documents, TAGS shows the labels on them. Same universe, same physics — reorganized around your own vocabulary.
  • ALL TAGS panel: the “ALL TAGS — 37” browser lays every pill out in one place — browse, filter and spotlight any tag's documents without hunting through menus.
  • Merge and rename everywhere: drag a pill onto another to merge duplicates, rename once to update every document — the map rewires itself around the change.
  • Project-tag panel: the side panel lists every member document of the selected tag — open any of them at the center of gravity without losing the web.

Why it matters for organization

Folders force one home per file; tags let a file live under every idea it touches — and the web shows you which labels overlap, which duplicate each other, and which documents bridge distant topics. New to a corpus? The shape of the web is the onboarding: dense clusters are the core themes, long tethers are the interdisciplinary surprises. Pair it with the thematic view for distilled ideas, or search any keyword and watch the matching bodies light the way. All computed on your machine, offline-first: your taxonomy never leaves your PC.
